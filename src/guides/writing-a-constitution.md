A constitution is the set of rules your group agrees to follow. It protects members, shows landlords and funders that your group is properly run, and helps settle disagreements fairly.

It doesn't need to be long or written in legal language. Plain English is best, so every member can understand it.

## What to include

### Name

The full name of your group, for example "Hillside Tenants' and Residents' Association".

### Aims

What your group exists to do. For example, to represent residents' views, improve the area, and bring the community together.

### Area

The streets, blocks or estate the group covers. A simple list or map reference is fine.

### Membership

- who can join, for example anyone aged 16 or over living in the area
- whether there's a membership fee
- how someone stops being a member

### Equal opportunities

A statement that the group is open to everyone in the area and won't discriminate against anyone.

### Committee and officers

- which roles the committee has, such as chair, secretary and treasurer
- how many committee members there can be
- how long people serve before standing for election again
- how vacancies are filled between elections

### Meetings

- how often the committee and general meetings take place
- how much notice members must be given
- the quorum: the minimum number of people needed for a meeting to make decisions
- how votes are taken

### Annual general meeting

When it happens, what it covers and how elections work. See our [guide to running an AGM](/guides/running-an-agm/).

### Money

- that the group will have a bank account in its name
- how many people must approve each payment (at least two)
- that accounts will be presented at every AGM

### Changing the constitution

How changes are agreed. Many groups require a vote at a general meeting with notice given in advance.

### Closing the group

What happens if the group decides to close, including what happens to any money left over.

## Before you adopt it

Share the draft with residents before the meeting where you vote on it. Once agreed, the chair and secretary should sign and date a copy, and the secretary should keep it safe.
