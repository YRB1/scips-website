A tenants' and residents' association (TRA) is a group of people from the same area who work together to improve it. This guide takes you through the main steps. You don't have to do it alone: SCIPS can support you at every stage.

## 1. Talk to a few neighbours

Before you organise anything formal, find two or three people who share your concerns. Ask them:

- what they like about living here
- what they'd most like to change
- whether they'd come to a meeting about it

A small, committed group is enough to get started.

## 2. Decide the area your group will cover

Agree which streets, blocks or estate the group is for. Keep it to an area where people share the same issues and can get to meetings easily. You can write the area down later in your constitution.

## 3. Hold a first open meeting

Invite everyone in the area. A letter through each door, a poster in a local shop or community centre, and word of mouth all help.

At the meeting:

- explain why you want to start a group
- ask people what matters to them
- agree whether to set up a group
- ask for volunteers to help get it started

Keep a note of who came and how to contact them, with their permission.

## 4. Agree a constitution

A constitution is your group's rulebook. It sets out what the group is for, who can join, and how decisions are made. Most landlords and funders will ask to see one. Read our [guide to writing a constitution](/guides/writing-a-constitution/).

## 5. Elect a committee

Most groups have at least a chair, a secretary and a treasurer, plus a few other committee members. Elections usually happen at your first general meeting and then at each annual general meeting.

## 6. Open a bank account

Open an account in the group's name, with at least two people needed to approve every payment. Read our [guide to managing your group's money](/guides/managing-your-groups-money/).

## 7. Introduce yourselves to your landlord and the council

Let your landlord and the council know your group exists, who your contacts are, and what you'd like to work on together. A good working relationship from the start makes everything easier.
